Real-Time Resource Allocation for Learned Multi-UAV Monitoring

[DRAFT TEXT 1 — one paragraph] In a hierarchical control loop for monitoring, a fleet-level resource has to be shared, in this case communication. A team of small UAVs tracks a spreading thermal field, such as a wildfire front: each vehicle flies a learned coverage policy, and an allocator above them decides each vehicle’s share of the budget, which sets its coverage radius. As the front moves and the demand shifts, the allocator does not always have time to work out the best split; sometimes it has to hand out a suboptimal allocation and move on. Even then, that allocation has to be feasible. Overstep the shared budget and the visual feeds drop, and in a monitoring task that means losing time-sensitive data. So we built a testbed that puts allocators through exactly this setting: baselines from dual decomposition, primal decomposition and online primal–dual methods, and our own allocator, which keeps every intermediate allocation feasible while the demand changes. We then flew the full stack on four Crazyflie quadrotors in a motion-capture arena.
